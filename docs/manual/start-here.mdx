# Start here

This manual is for the people doing the mahi — taking enquiries, visiting
whānau, and writing up what happened.

It is organised by job, not by the order things happen in. Find your job below
and read that part. The rest is somebody else's work, and you can safely ignore
it.

## Which one are you?

| If your job is… | You are probably a… | Start with |
|---|---|---|
| Taking enquiries as they arrive, recording who the person is, and sending them on | **Intake officer** | [Your day at a glance](/manual/intake-officer/your-day-at-a-glance) |
| Visiting and supporting the people assigned to you, and writing up what you did | **<Term path="caseWorker" />** | [Your day at a glance](/manual/case-worker/your-day-at-a-glance) |
| Overseeing a team, approving what comes in, and deciding who picks up what | **Supervisor** | [What a supervisor can do](/manual/supervisor/what-you-can-do) |
| Watching the whole organisation rather than one team | **Program manager** | [What a program manager can do](/manual/program-manager/what-you-can-do) |

Not sure? Whatever your job title is, the system decides what you can see from
the positions you hold on services. The quickest test is to open the system and
look at the menu down the left — [finding your way
around](/manual/everyone/finding-your-way-around) explains what you are seeing.

If you do more than one of these jobs, you get one dashboard rather than
several: the system shows the one with the widest access. The other sections
still apply to you, so read whichever matches the task in front of you.

A few things are the same for everybody, whatever your job. Those are under
[Everyone](/manual/everyone/signing-in): signing in, finding your way around,
and what to do when something looks wrong.

## What this system is for

Manaaki Central keeps one record of everyone your organisation is supporting.

When someone new comes to you, you enter them once. From then on, the system
holds who they are, who else is involved in their life, what service they are
getting, what you have done with them, and what still needs doing by when.

The point is that nothing lives only in your head or your notebook. If you are
away, someone else can pick it up. When funders ask what you did, the answer is
already recorded.

## The shape of the work

Almost everything follows the same path. Different people own different steps,
which is why the manual is split the way it is.

1. **Someone is referred to you** — by another agency, by their whānau, or they
   walk in. *(Intake officer)*
2. **You decide what happens next** — accept them into a service, or send it to
   a manager to look at first. *(Intake officer, supervisor)*
3. **Work starts** — a <Term path="serviceEpisode" /> opens, and someone is
   assigned to walk alongside them. *(Supervisor assigns; <Term path="caseWorker" /> starts)*
4. **You record what you do** — visits, phone calls, and notes. *(<Term path="caseWorker" />)*
5. **Deadlines are tracked** — consent forms, assessments, and paperwork, with
   dates. *(<Term path="caseWorker" />)*
6. **The work closes** — you write it up and finish. *(<Term path="caseWorker" />, usually with a supervisor)*

## If you get stuck

Every page ends with what to do if the screen does not look like the picture.
There is also a page on [when something looks
wrong](/manual/everyone/when-something-looks-wrong) that collects the common ones.

One thing worth knowing early: when you are taking on someone new, the
**<Term path="referral" />** is not saved until you finish the first step and
click **Continue**. After that it is safe to leave and come back. Before that,
closing the page loses what you typed.

The person's own record is different — if you add somebody new part-way
through, they are created as soon as you submit that little form, and they
stay even if you abandon the rest.
