# Safety alerts

**By the end of this page** you will know how to raise a safety alert, how a
manager decides on one, where alerts show, who can see them, and how to ask
another service to share theirs.

Each video is under a minute. Watch the ones that match your job.

<Callout color="amber" title="Only raise an alert if it changes how the work is done">
A safety alert warns the team working with a whānau about a risk to staff.
</Callout>

## 1. What a safety alert is

The three levels, the raise form, and what happens after you raise one.

- **Low** — be aware.
- **Medium** — take the precautions.
- **High** — serious risk, follow every precaution.

<Video id="I_pK1CGvCMI" title="What a safety alert is" />

## 2. Raise an alert

A <Term path="caseWorker" /> raises an alert from the <Term path="serviceEpisode" />.
A manager of the service must approve it. Until then, staff on the
<Term path="serviceEpisode" /> see it as awaiting approval.

## 6. Decline an alert

Decline with a short reason. The reason is saved. Colleagues do not see a
declined alert.

<Video id="Y4m_LJhSeDc" title="Decline an alert" />

## 7. Who sees what

If you are allocated to the <Term path="serviceEpisode" />, you see the full
alert: the level, the precautions, and why it was raised. Other services only
see that an alert exists, and which service holds it. They can
[ask for access](#10-ask-another-service-for-access).

<Video id="2XOiFNaOpYY" title="Who sees what" />

## 8. Where alerts show

On the <Term path="serviceEpisode" /> overview, on the client page, and as a
marker next to the person's name in lists.

<Video id="-0ZtXY4rU-Y" title="Where alerts show" />

## 9. End an alert

When the risk has passed, a manager of the service ends the alert and gives a
reason. Only an active alert can be ended. An alert still waiting for approval
is declined instead.

<Video id="kY5oC5mqddk" title="End an alert" />

## 10. Ask another service for access

When another service has an approved alert for the person, a notice at the top
of your <Term path="serviceEpisode" /> names that service. It does not say what
the alert is about.

<Screen
  src="/img/manual/safety-alerts/05-other-service-notice.png"
  alt="A notice saying another service has a safety alert for this person, with a Request access button"
  caption="The notice names the service, nothing more."
/>

If you can raise alerts on this <Term path="serviceEpisode" />, the notice has a
**Request access** button. If you can't, it asks you to contact that service.

1. Press **Request access**.
2. Add a short note on why you need it. This is optional.
3. Press **Request access**.

<Screen
  src="/img/manual/safety-alerts/06-request-access-dialog.png"
  alt="The Request access to safety alert window, with an optional notes box"
  caption="Your note is shown to the manager who decides."
  narrow
/>

A manager of the service that raised the alert decides. The notice changes to
**Access requested · Pending**. If more than one service has an alert for the
person, one request goes to all of them, and each decides on its own alerts.

- **If they approve**, the full alert appears on your
  <Term path="serviceEpisode" />: the level, the precautions, and why it was
  raised.
- **If they decline**, you still see only the notice. You can ask again.

## 11. Decide on an access request

Requests for alerts your service raised are on your dashboard, under **Safety
Alert Access Requests**. The table only appears when a request is waiting. Only
a manager of the service that raised the alert can decide.

1. Press **Review** next to the request.
2. Check who is asking, their note, and the precautions. Open **Individual alert
   details** to see each alert in full.
3. Press **Approve** or **Decline**. Your decision is saved straight away.

<Screen
  src="/img/manual/safety-alerts/07-review-access-request.png"
  alt="The access request review window: who asked, when, their note and the precautions on the left; Approve and Decline on the right"
  caption="Approve shares the alert details. Decline keeps the alert private."
/>

One request can cover several alerts. **Approve** or **Decline** answers all of
them at once. If you decline, the other service can ask again.

<Callout color="amber" title="Approving cannot be undone">
Once you approve, the other service sees the full alert on their
<Term path="serviceEpisode" /> for this person. There is no way to take access
back.
</Callout>
